# THE CONSTITUTION OF WITNESS

## Preamble

Witness exists because people are too often invited to participate without being given meaningful
power over what follows.

Communities share their experiences. Participants attend workshops. Facilitators collect stories,
photographs, recordings, observations, priorities, and ideas. These contributions are then condensed
into reports, removed from their original context, interpreted by people with greater institutional
authority, and separated from those who provided them.

The people who contributed may never see what was recorded, how it was understood, what decision it
influenced, or whether anything changed.

Witness is founded to interrupt that pattern.

Witness shall provide trusted digital infrastructure for participatory design, community
consultation, collective sensemaking, and accountable decision-making. It shall allow people to
contribute in ways that are culturally appropriate, accessible, traceable, and governed by
meaningful consent.

It shall not treat people merely as sources of data. It shall recognise them as participants in
knowledge creation and, wherever possible, as continuing authorities over their own contributions.

This Constitution establishes the purpose, principles, rights, powers, limitations, and
non-negotiable obligations of Witness.

---

## Article I — Name and Identity

### Section 1. Name

The name of the system shall be **Witness**.

### Section 2. Meaning

To witness is more than to record.

To witness is to:

1. be present;
2. listen with care;
3. preserve context;
4. acknowledge whose knowledge is being shared;
5. recognise the conditions under which it was shared;
6. make visible how it is interpreted;
7. connect it to decisions and action; and
8. remain accountable to the person or community from whom it came.

Witness shall embody this meaning throughout its technology, governance, language, and institutional
practice.

### Section 3. Character

Witness shall be:

* a co-design and participatory evidence platform;
* a consent-aware system of record;
* a tool for transparent synthesis and decision-making;
* an accountability mechanism between communities and institutions;
* adaptable to different cultural and organisational settings;
* capable of operating in low-connectivity environments;
* open to inspection and responsible adaptation; and
* governed as public-interest infrastructure rather than an instrument of extraction or surveillance.

---

## Article II — Purpose

Witness shall enable communities, facilitators, researchers, programme teams, governments,
civil-society organisations, and other authorised participants to undertake a transparent evidence
lifecycle:

1. create a co-design activity or engagement;
2. explain its purpose, participants, boundaries, and intended use;
3. establish appropriate consent;
4. capture contributions and contextual information;
5. allow contributors to review or correct material where practicable;
6. organise contributions without destroying their provenance;
7. develop themes, findings, and candidate insights;
8. distinguish human testimony from interpretation and automated suggestion;
9. validate interpretations with appropriate participants;
10. record decisions and the evidence that informed them;
11. communicate outcomes back to contributors;
12. preserve a trustworthy audit history; and
13. manage retention, withdrawal, restriction, archival, or deletion according to the applicable
    governance agreement.

Witness succeeds only when participation can be followed through to understanding, decision, action,
and accountability.

---

## Article III — Foundational Principles

### Section 1. Human dignity

Every participant shall be treated as a person with dignity, history, relationships, and agency—not
as a datapoint.

### Section 2. Free, prior, informed, and continuing consent

Consent shall be understandable, specific, recorded, and capable of being revisited.

Consent shall not be presumed merely because a person attended an activity, entered a room,
completed a form, or previously agreed to another use.

Where a new use materially exceeds the original purpose, renewed consent or another legitimate
governance decision shall be required.

### Section 3. Data minimisation

Witness shall collect only what is necessary for a clearly stated purpose.

The possibility of collecting information shall never, by itself, justify its collection.

### Section 4. Provenance

Every material contribution, interpretation, synthesis, and decision shall retain sufficient
provenance to answer:

* Where did this come from?
* Who contributed or created it?
* When and under what conditions was it created?
* What consent and restrictions apply?
* Has it been changed?
* Who changed it?
* How was it used?
* What decision, if any, did it influence?

### Section 5. Context

A contribution shall not be separated from the context necessary to understand it responsibly.

Translation, summarisation, coding, redaction, categorisation, and aggregation shall be treated as
interpretive acts and recorded accordingly.

### Section 6. Participation over extraction

Witness shall not define successful participation by the volume of information collected.

Its measure shall be whether people were heard faithfully, could influence interpretation,
understood how their contribution would be used, and could see what followed.

### Section 7. Transparency

The operation of the system, the authority of its users, and the history of material actions shall
be understandable and inspectable by appropriately authorised people.

### Section 8. Accountability

Every consequential action shall be attributable to an authorised actor, policy, or recorded process.

No institution shall use technological complexity to avoid responsibility for a decision.

### Section 9. Inclusion and accessibility

Witness shall be designed for differing languages, literacies, abilities, devices, bandwidth
conditions, cultural protocols, and levels of digital confidence.

Offline and low-connectivity participation shall be treated as core conditions, not exceptional cases.

### Section 10. Cultural legitimacy

Witness shall support locally determined protocols concerning knowledge, identity, representation,
custodianship, collective consent, sacred or restricted information, and authority to speak.

The platform shall not assume that all knowledge is individually owned, universally shareable, or
appropriately governed by Western concepts of data ownership.

### Section 11. Security and proportionality

Security shall protect participants and their contributions without creating unnecessary barriers to
participation.

The more sensitive the material and the greater the possible harm, the stronger the required
safeguards shall be.

### Section 12. Openness without exposure

Witness should be open in its code, standards, documentation, and governance wherever practicable.

Openness of infrastructure shall never mean openness of personal, sensitive, culturally restricted,
or community-governed information.

---

## Article IV — Rights of Participants

Every participant shall, subject to legitimate and clearly documented limitations, have the right:

1. to understand the purpose of an engagement before contributing;
2. to know who is conducting and funding it;
3. to know who may access their contribution;
4. to choose whether and how to participate;
5. to decline without coercion or retaliation;
6. to contribute anonymously or pseudonymously where appropriate;
7. to specify restrictions on attribution, publication, quotation, and reuse;
8. to review, correct, clarify, or contextualise their contribution;
9. to withdraw consent for future use where withdrawal is legally and operationally possible;
10. to request restriction, redaction, or deletion according to the governing agreement;
11. to know when automated systems have assisted with transcription, translation, classification,
    summarisation, or analysis;
12. not to be represented by an automated inference as though it were their own statement;
13. to challenge an interpretation attributed to them or their community;
14. to know what findings were produced;
15. to know what decisions were made;
16. to receive an explanation when their contribution materially influenced a decision;
17. to be informed when no action is taken;
18. to raise concerns and seek remedy; and
19. to participate without surrendering rights beyond those expressly and knowingly granted.

These rights shall shape the system’s architecture and workflow. They shall not exist only as
statements in a policy document.

---

## Article V — Responsibilities of Participants and Practitioners

Participants shall be expected to respect the dignity, privacy, safety, and cultural rights of others.

Facilitators, researchers, administrators, designers, analysts, and institutional users shall:

1. state the purpose and boundaries of each engagement truthfully;
2. collect the minimum necessary information;
3. avoid coercive or misleading consent practices;
4. preserve context and provenance;
5. distinguish observation from interpretation;
6. identify uncertainty and disagreement;
7. prevent unauthorised disclosure;
8. avoid presenting minority views as consensus;
9. avoid presenting institutional interpretation as community voice;
10. return findings to participants where safe and practicable;
11. document decisions and unresolved concerns;
12. use elevated access only for legitimate purposes; and
13. accept accountability for how Witness is configured and used.

No technical feature shall relieve a practitioner or institution of these responsibilities.

---

## Article VI — Contributions, Evidence, and Interpretation

### Section 1. Contributions

A contribution may include text, speech, image, video, drawing, document, vote, observation,
artefact, annotation, location, or other authorised form of expression.

### Section 2. Original record

Where safe and consented to, Witness shall preserve an original or source record so that later
transformations can be traced.

### Section 3. Derived material

Transcriptions, translations, summaries, codes, themes, findings, recommendations, and decisions
shall be stored as derived material connected to—not substituted for—their sources.

### Section 4. Candidate findings

Machine-generated or preliminary findings shall be clearly marked as candidates.

A candidate finding shall not become an accepted finding merely because it was generated
confidently, repeated frequently, or produced by an advanced model.

### Section 5. Validation

Consequential findings shall undergo an appropriate validation process. Depending on the engagement,
this may include:

* participant review;
* community validation;
* facilitator review;
* subject-matter review;
* cultural review;
* triangulation with other evidence; or
* formal approval by an authorised governance body.

### Section 6. Disagreement

Witness shall preserve legitimate disagreement.

The system shall not manufacture consensus by erasing dissenting, minority, uncertain, or
inconvenient perspectives.

### Section 7. Evidentiary chain

Where a finding informs a recommendation or decision, Witness should preserve a traceable chain:

**Contribution → interpretation → finding → validation → decision → action → reported outcome.**

---

## Article VII — Consent and Information Governance

### Section 1. Consent as a governed record

Consent shall be stored as a versioned and auditable record linked to the relevant participant,
contribution, purpose, and permitted use.

### Section 2. Changes in consent

Changes to consent shall not silently rewrite history. The system shall preserve when consent
changed, what changed, who initiated the change, and which future uses are affected.

### Section 3. Collective and customary authority

Where knowledge belongs to, concerns, or could harm a family, clan, community, organisation, or
other collective, individual consent alone may be insufficient.

Witness shall support governance processes that recognise legitimate collective or customary
authority without extinguishing individual rights.

### Section 4. Purpose limitation

Information collected for one engagement shall not automatically become available for unrelated
research, commercial activity, model training, public release, or institutional surveillance.

### Section 5. Sensitive and restricted knowledge

Witness shall permit information to be classified and governed according to its sensitivity,
including information that is:

* personal;
* confidential;
* culturally restricted;
* sacred;
* commercially sensitive;
* politically sensitive;
* related to children or vulnerable people;
* capable of exposing a person or community to retaliation; or
* restricted by law, agreement, or community protocol.

### Section 6. Retention and deletion

Every engagement shall establish a retention policy.

Information shall not be retained indefinitely by default. Deletion, archival, anonymisation, and
preservation requirements shall be documented and technically enforceable.

---

## Article VIII — Authority and Access

### Section 1. Least privilege

Every person and service shall receive only the access required for its legitimate function.

### Section 2. Separation of authority

The ability to collect information shall not automatically grant the ability to analyse, export,
publish, administer, or delete it.

### Section 3. Role-based and policy-based control

Witness shall use enforceable role and policy controls so that authority depends upon the actor,
action, resource, engagement, consent conditions, and relevant governance rules.

### Section 4. Privileged actions

Administrative, export, publication, consent-override, access-policy, retention, and deletion
actions shall require enhanced authorisation and shall be auditable.

### Section 5. Denial by default

When authority is unclear, access shall be denied until legitimacy is established.

### Section 6. No invisible superuser

No person shall possess unreviewable authority over all Witness information.

Emergency or exceptional access, where necessary, shall be narrow, time-bound, recorded, and subject
to subsequent review.

---

## Article IX — Artificial Intelligence and Automation

Witness may use artificial intelligence to assist with transcription, translation, organisation,
retrieval, comparison, summarisation, accessibility, and the identification of possible patterns.

Artificial intelligence shall remain subordinate to human and community authority.

Accordingly:

1. AI output shall be identifiable as AI-assisted or AI-generated;
2. source material shall remain distinguishable from generated interpretation;
3. confidence shall not be represented as truth;
4. material uncertainty shall be visible;
5. consequential findings shall require human review;
6. culturally significant interpretation shall require culturally legitimate review;
7. participants shall not be assigned motives, identities, risks, or beliefs without a legitimate
   and reviewable basis;
8. private contributions shall not be used for external model training without explicit authority;
9. automated processing shall respect consent and access restrictions;
10. AI shall not make final decisions affecting rights, eligibility, safety, funding, or
    representation; and
11. the institution deploying the system shall remain accountable for every decision made with its assistance.

Witness shall help people interpret evidence. It shall not replace people as the authors of
community meaning.

---

## Article X — Integrity, Security, and Auditability

Witness shall maintain trustworthy records of material system events.

The integrity framework shall include:

* authenticated identity where appropriate;
* explicit authorisation;
* tamper-evident audit history;
* traceable changes;
* secure handling of credentials and secrets;
* encryption appropriate to risk;
* controlled exports;
* protected backups;
* tested restoration;
* dependency and supply-chain safeguards;
* vulnerability management;
* incident response;
* data-breach procedures;
* separation between public and restricted material; and
* regular verification that safeguards operate as designed.

Audit records shall serve accountability, not surveillance. They shall record what is necessary to
establish responsibility while avoiding unnecessary exposure of participants.

Where integrity cannot be verified, the system shall say so plainly.

---

## Article XI — Architecture and Technical Sovereignty

Witness shall be developed as modular, inspectable, and portable infrastructure.

Its architecture should:

1. separate the domain rules from presentation and infrastructure;
2. use explicit and versioned contracts;
3. prevent silent changes to constitutional behaviour;
4. support interoperable and documented data formats;
5. avoid unnecessary dependence on a single vendor;
6. permit responsible self-hosting and regional hosting;
7. support low-bandwidth and intermittently connected environments;
8. provide reliable health and readiness indicators;
9. maintain safe migration and rollback paths;
10. keep core workflows usable without avoidable third-party egress;
11. preserve the ability to export governed information in usable formats; and
12. ensure that communities and institutions are not trapped inside the platform.

Open-source technology shall be preferred where it advances transparency, sustainability, security,
adaptability, and local capacity.

Technical sophistication shall never become an excuse for institutional dependence.

---

## Article XII — Governance of the Project

### Section 1. Stewardship

Witness shall be governed by stewardship rather than ownership alone.

Stewards shall protect its constitutional purpose, maintain its infrastructure, support its
communities, and prevent its capture by interests inconsistent with this Constitution.

### Section 2. Distributed responsibilities

Architecture, product, design, engineering, security, quality assurance, research, community
engagement, data governance, operations, and ethics shall each have defined responsibilities.

No department, agent, maintainer, donor, customer, or founder may unilaterally override the
Constitution merely because they control funding, infrastructure, or implementation.

### Section 3. Decision records

Material decisions shall be documented with:

* the question considered;
* the evidence available;
* the people or roles consulted;
* the decision made;
* the reasons for it;
* the risks and dissenting views;
* the accountable owner; and
* the conditions under which it should be reviewed.

### Section 4. Changes to the system

Significant changes shall identify their effect on:

* participant rights;
* consent;
* access;
* privacy;
* cultural governance;
* security;
* interoperability;
* accessibility;
* offline use;
* institutional accountability; and
* the constitutional principles of Witness.

### Section 5. Development agents

Human and automated development agents shall operate within defined charters.

An agent may recommend, design, implement, test, or review only within its authorised scope.
Automated agents shall not silently establish product policy, weaken safeguards, or redefine
participant rights through implementation choices.

### Section 6. Quality gates

A feature shall not be considered complete merely because its code executes.

Completion requires evidence appropriate to its risk, including tests, security review,
accessibility consideration, documentation, operational readiness, and confirmation that
constitutional requirements remain satisfied.

---

## Article XIII — Prohibited Uses

Witness shall not knowingly be designed, configured, or used to:

1. conduct covert surveillance;
2. identify or punish dissent;
3. coerce participation;
4. fabricate community consent;
5. launder institutional decisions as community-led outcomes;
6. extract cultural knowledge for unauthorised commercial use;
7. expose vulnerable people or communities;
8. rank human worth or legitimacy;
9. infer sensitive characteristics without a lawful, necessary, and ethically defensible basis;
10. train external AI systems on participant material without explicit authority;
11. manipulate testimony or erase material disagreement;
12. provide unrestricted access merely for administrative convenience;
13. retain information without purpose or limit;
14. export governed information beyond its authorised context; or
15. conceal breaches, alterations, automated processing, or conflicts of interest.

A deployment substantially dependent upon any prohibited use shall not be recognised as a legitimate
implementation of Witness.

---

## Article XIV — Accountability and Remedy

Every Witness deployment shall provide a visible process through which participants can:

* ask how their information is being used;
* challenge inaccurate records or interpretations;
* report harm or misuse;
* request review of an access or consent decision;
* seek correction, restriction, or other remedy; and
* receive a reasoned response.

Serious incidents shall be documented, investigated, contained, and communicated to affected people
where safe and required.

Remedy may include correction, annotation, restriction, deletion, restored access, renewed
consultation, withdrawal of a finding, public clarification, technical repair, governance reform, or
other action proportionate to the harm.

The preservation of an audit trail shall never take precedence over preventing continuing harm.

---

## Article XV — Measures of Success

Witness shall not measure success primarily through registrations, contributions collected,
recordings stored, dashboards created, or reports generated.

It shall assess whether:

1. participants understood the engagement;
2. consent was meaningful;
3. underrepresented people could participate;
4. contributions retained their context;
5. interpretations were validated;
6. disagreement remained visible;
7. decisions could be traced to evidence;
8. institutions responded to what they heard;
9. outcomes were returned to participants;
10. participants could exercise their rights;
11. harms were prevented or remedied;
12. local people gained capability and authority; and
13. the system strengthened trust rather than merely collecting information.

---

## Article XVI — Constitutional Invariants

The following commitments are non-negotiable and shall be enforced wherever technically possible:

1. No contribution without an identifiable purpose.
2. No sensitive use without appropriate consent or legitimate authority.
3. No interpretation presented as original testimony.
4. No accepted finding without accountable validation.
5. No consequential decision without traceable authorship.
6. No privileged access without an auditable basis.
7. No silent alteration of evidence, consent, or history.
8. No export beyond applicable permissions.
9. No AI-generated claim represented as community truth.
10. No deployment may call itself participatory while preventing participants from seeing what
    resulted from their participation.

These invariants shall guide domain rules, contracts, database constraints, authorisation policies,
tests, user interfaces, operational procedures, and release gates.

---

## Article XVII — Amendment

This Constitution may evolve as Witness learns from the communities it serves.

An amendment shall require:

1. a written proposal;
2. an explanation of the problem being addressed;
3. an assessment of its effect on participant rights and constitutional safeguards;
4. consultation with affected stakeholders;
5. documentation of objections and alternative proposals;
6. approval through the established governance process;
7. a versioned public record of the change; and
8. a migration plan where existing engagements or information are affected.

No amendment may retrospectively manufacture consent or legitimise a use that was unauthorised when
it occurred.

The rights to dignity, meaningful consent, provenance, accountable interpretation, security, and
remedy shall not be removed for commercial expediency or technical convenience.

---

## Article XVIII — Founding Commitment

Witness shall remember that the people closest to a problem are not merely research subjects. They
are holders of knowledge, interpreters of experience, and potential authors of the response.

The platform shall therefore be built so that people can see themselves in the record, recognise how
meaning was made, and follow their contribution into action.

Witness shall not promise that every contribution will determine an outcome.

It shall promise that participation will be treated honestly; that evidence will not be silently
manipulated; that authority will be visible; that consent and context will matter; and that
institutions using the system can be called to account.

This is the covenant of Witness:

**What is shared shall be treated with care.
What is changed shall remain traceable.
What is inferred shall not be confused with what was said.
What is decided shall be attributable.
What is promised shall be followed.
And those who contribute shall not disappear from the story their knowledge helped create.**
